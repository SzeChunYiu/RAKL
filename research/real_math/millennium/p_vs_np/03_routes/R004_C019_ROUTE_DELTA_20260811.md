C019 selects the quadratic-residue difference relation on `Z_p` for primes `p == 3 mod 8`:

`QR_p(x,y)=1  iff  y-x is a nonzero quadratic residue mod p`.

This is the Paley-tournament relation viewed as a square bipartite graph.

## Admission result

C019 proves the `p x p` adjacency matrix is invertible over `GF(2)`, hence has rank exactly `p`.

The proof uses the circulant polynomial `a(X)=sum_{q in Q_p}X^q`. For a primitive `p`-th root `omega` in characteristic two, the quadratic-residue period `eta_Q` satisfies

`eta_Q^2 = eta_N`,
`eta_Q + eta_N = 1`,

because `2` is a quadratic nonresidue when `p == 3 mod 8`. Thus `eta_Q^2+eta_Q+1=0`, so neither period vanishes. Together with `a(1)=1`, the circulant has no zero on any `p`-th root and is invertible.

C019 also proves all rows and columns are distinct. Therefore no exact C018 arbitrary-pullback representation can compress the graph to fewer than `p` left/right feature vertices by identifying rows or columns.

Density gives degree `(p-1)/2` and an `Omega(p)` lower bound on forest-partition count, so the merged bounded-degree and constant-arboricity screens do not yield logarithmic ceilings for this family.

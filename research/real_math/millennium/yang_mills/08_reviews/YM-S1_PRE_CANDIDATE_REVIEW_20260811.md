## 1. Transfer-matrix / operator-theory lead

**Background/role:** reflection-positive lattice systems, positive self-adjoint transfer matrices, spectral measures, semigroup correlation functions, thermodynamic spectral limits.

**Finding:** the first logical bridge is not "prove exponential decay" but "prove the tested sources can see every sub-gap excitation." For a positive transfer matrix, a Euclidean correlator sees only the spectral measure generated by its source vector. A state orthogonal to every tested source can lie below the observed decay rate without contradiction.

**Strongest objection:** statements of the form "correlations decay like `e^{-mt}`, therefore the Hamiltonian gap is at least `m`" are false unless the correlation family is cyclic/dense/spectrally complete in the relevant physical subspace or an equivalent visibility theorem is supplied.

**Delegated check:** after the pre-candidate gate passes, construct the smallest exact positive finite-dimensional transfer-matrix example with energies `0 < ε < m` in which the tested observable couples the vacuum to the `m` state but not the `ε` state. This is the cheapest regression test for every later clustering-to-gap argument.

**Vote:** `ACCEPT` `YM-S1a` as first discriminator; `BLOCK` any full-gap inference from restricted correlations before visibility is bound.

## 2. Lattice gauge / strong-coupling lead

**Background/role:** Wilson lattice actions, character/cluster expansions, strong-coupling analyticity, gauge-invariant observables, infinite-volume lattice measures.

**Finding:** strong-coupling control is real and valuable but lives at fixed cutoff and restricted bare-coupling regimes. Osterwalder–Seiler supplies transfer-matrix positivity and confinement-side control; Shen–Zhu–Zhu supplies strong-coupling functional inequalities and exponential correlation decay for a broad class. Neither source, as used in this packet, proves the root continuum gap.

**Strongest objection:** an `a`-independent lattice-unit decay rate is not the target quantity. Along a continuum trajectory one must track physical units and the bare coupling leaves the proved strong-coupling window.

**Delegated check:** every future strong-coupling bound must expose (i) exact observable class, (ii) dependence on coupling, dimension, gauge group and volume, and (iii) its `a`-dependence after a continuum trajectory is specified.

**Vote:** `REVISE` strong-coupling-to-continuum routes until `G3`, `G5`, and `G6` are explicit.

## 3. Renormalization-group / asymptotic-freedom lead

**Background/role:** multiscale lattice RG, asymptotic freedom, effective actions, weak-bare-coupling continuum scaling, quantitative stability across blocking steps.

**Finding:** Balaban-type UV control and strong-coupling IR gap estimates are complementary but currently separated by a missing comparison law. The research object should be a **gap-sensitive quantity with an RG transport inequality**, not a narrative that "RG flows to strong coupling."

**Strongest objection:** without a theorem controlling how a spectral/correlation quantity transforms under each blocking step, the coupling flow alone does not transport a positive lower bound. Reflection positivity and source visibility may also be lost or obscured by the chosen effective representation.

**Delegated check:** for any later `YM-S1c` candidate, write a one-step blocking identity/inequality before attempting many-scale iteration. Require every error term to be explicit and audit whether the gap-sensitive quantity remains in the same observable/Hilbert-space interpretation.

**Vote:** `BLOCK` direct weak-to-strong interpolation claims; `ACCEPT` `G5` as a separate child atom.

## 4. Confinement / center-sector lead

**Background/role:** Wilson/'t Hooft observables, center symmetry, higher-form sectors, area laws, screening and confinement criteria.

**Finding:** Wilson-loop area law is not a universal synonym for the physical mass gap. The recent primary literature is especially useful as a directionality check: Cao–Nissim–Sheffield derive area-law results from a mass-gap condition in a dynamical route, while Nissim's 2026 `SO(3)` result shows Wilson's confinement criterion can fail even at strong coupling for a compact simple group with trivial center.

**Strongest objection:** center-sensitive extended observables can miss center-neutral glueball-like excitations, and the Clay statement quantifies over compact simple gauge groups rather than only groups where a Wilson criterion is the natural confinement diagnostic.

**Delegated check:** require every confinement-to-gap proposal to name the physical sector it controls and the theorem that maps that sector to the complete low-energy spectrum. If no such map exists, record confinement as orthogonal evidence, not as a gap certificate.

**Vote:** `BLOCK` area-law-as-gap inference; `ACCEPT` area law as an adversarial calibration surface.

## 5. Constructive-QFT / OS reconstruction lead

**Background/role:** Euclidean field theory, reflection positivity, Schwinger functions, OS reconstruction, continuum and thermodynamic limits.

**Finding:** even a correct lattice spectral lemma does not close the root unless the same observable theory converges to a nontrivial continuum OS/Wightman theory and the spectral lower bound is identified in that reconstruction. `YM-S1` is therefore downstream-coupled to the constructive lane but should not duplicate it.

**Strongest objection:** spectral gaps are not automatically stable under weak convergence when Hilbert spaces, vacuum sectors, or observable algebras vary with the regulator. A limit can lose spectral information or become trivial.

**Delegated check:** `YM-S1d` must state the exact convergence/reconstruction theorem used to pass from lattice correlators/transfer matrices to the continuum Hamiltonian. "Take `a -> 0`" is not an admissible proof step.

**Vote:** `REVISE`; spectral work may proceed locally but root authority remains blocked on the existence/reconstruction branch.

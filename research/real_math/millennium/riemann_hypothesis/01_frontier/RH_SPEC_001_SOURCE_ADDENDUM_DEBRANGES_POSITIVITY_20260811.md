## 1. Suzuki canonical-system criterion

**Masatoshi Suzuki, _A canonical system of differential equations arising from the Riemann zeta-function_, arXiv:1204.1827 (2012).**

Suzuki studies

`Theta_omega(z) = xi(1/2 - omega - i z) / xi(1/2 + omega - i z)`, `omega > 0`.

The paper gives necessary/sufficient conditions related to `Theta_omega` being a meromorphic inner function and constructs the associated de Branges canonical system explicitly and unconditionally for `omega > 1`. The paper states that an unconditional extension of the construction to every `omega > 0` yields an RH criterion in which the corresponding Hamiltonians are positive semidefinite.

### Exact transfer lesson

This is a substantially sharper de Branges/canonical-system comparator than a generic “Hilbert space of entire functions” analogy. It supplies a concrete parameterized family and a known unconditional region.

### Load-bearing disanalogy / warning

The desired all-`omega` positivity is part of an RH criterion. Therefore simply writing down a canonical system or declaring its Hamiltonian positive would risk renaming the target. Any use in a strict candidate must separate:

1. unconditional existence of the canonical system;
2. positivity/semidefiniteness of the Hamiltonian;
3. the parameter range in `omega`;
4. boundary/limit conditions;
5. which implication is already equivalent to RH.

The high-information repair question is not “can de Branges theory express RH?”—it can. It is: **is there an unconditional structural mechanism that continues the canonical-system construction/positivity from the proved region into the full required parameter range without importing the RH-equivalent innerness/zero-location condition?**

## 2. Connes–Consani archimedean Weil positivity

**Alain Connes and Caterina Consani, _Weil positivity and Trace formula, the archimedean place_, arXiv:2006.13771 (2020).**

The paper gives a Hilbert-space explanation of Weil-functional positivity in the single archimedean-place setting. The positivity mechanism is tied to the trace of the scaling action compressed to the orthogonal complement of cutoff-projection ranges (the Sonin-space setting), and the difference between the Weil distribution and the Sonin trace is controlled using prolate spheroidal functions and Hermitian Toeplitz matrices. The authors state that the ingredients extend conceptually to the general semi-local setting, where Weil positivity would imply RH.

### Exact transfer lesson

The “Hilbert norm / energy factorization” analogy in the frozen context is not only an engineering analogy: there is a serious arithmetic operator programme in which a **local component** of the desired positivity has an explicit compression/trace mechanism.

### Load-bearing disanalogy / warning

The single archimedean-place result is not the full global Weil positivity theorem. A strict route must identify the exact finite-place/semi-local compatibility, limiting, trace, and correction-term obligations needed to assemble the global arithmetic functional. Local positivity does not compose automatically into global RH authority.

The high-information repair question is: **what is the smallest exact obstruction to globalizing the archimedean compression/positivity mechanism to a semi-local family whose arithmetic trace equals the full Weil functional on the registered admissible test class?**

## 3. Connes–Moscovici prolate spectral calibration

**Alain Connes and Henri Moscovici, _Prolate spheroidal operator and Zeta_, arXiv:2112.05500 (2021).**

The paper studies a self-adjoint extension of a prolate spheroidal operator. Its spectral features reproduce the ultraviolet behavior of the squares of Riemann-zero ordinates and connect to the Sonin-space framework.

### Exact transfer lesson

This is a useful hostile calibration for the bridge matrix: even a legitimate self-adjoint operator with striking zeta-related spectral asymptotics does not, by that fact alone, supply the exact multiset correspondence needed for Hilbert–Polya.

The matrix must therefore keep **self-adjointness** and **exact zero correspondence** as independent rows. Passing one cannot compensate for failure of the other.

## 4. de Branges primary route anchors

Louis de Branges' Purdue publication list records, among the primary operator-theoretic route papers:

- _The Riemann hypothesis for Hilbert spaces of entire functions_, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 15 (1986), 1–17;
- _A conjecture which implies the Riemann hypothesis_, J. Functional Analysis 121 (1994), 117–184.

These are retained as historical primary anchors. This addendum makes no claim that the conjectural condition in the latter paper is proved, nor that de Branges theory by itself closes RH.

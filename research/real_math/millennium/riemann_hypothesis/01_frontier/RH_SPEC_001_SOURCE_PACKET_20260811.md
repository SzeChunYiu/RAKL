# RH-SPEC-001 source packet — spectral/operator context

Accessed 2026-08-11. This packet supplies context and method-transfer anchors only. It is not a proof, novelty certificate, or independent review.

## 1. Exact root and Weil criterion

**E. Bombieri, _The Riemann Hypothesis_, Clay Mathematics Institute official problem description.**

The official formulation states RH as the assertion that the nontrivial zeros of `zeta(s)` have real part `1/2`, equivalently that the zeros of the completed `Xi(t)` are real. Bombieri also presents Weil's explicit formula and the equivalent sign criterion for convolution-type test functions. This is the strongest exact positivity bridge used in `RH-SPEC-001`: an operator or Hilbert-space factorization must represent the exact RH-equivalent functional, not a statistical surrogate.

Bombieri also emphasizes the finite-field analogy: zeta zeros are represented through Frobenius acting on cohomology, and the Riemann-Hypothesis part of the Weil conjectures is proved in that setting. The transfer is structural only; the classical problem does not inherit such a cohomology theory for free.

## 2. Explicit formula

**A. Weil, _Sur les « formules explicites » de la théorie des nombres premiers_ (1952).**

Primary source for the explicit-formula/positivity viewpoint used as the exact arithmetic target. The spectral lane treats Weil's criterion as a theorem-level specification: any proposed Hilbert norm, positive operator, trace formula, or kernel must be shown to equal the registered arithmetic functional on its full admissible class.

## 3. Solved trace-formula analogue

**A. Selberg, _Harmonic Analysis and Discontinuous Groups in Weakly Symmetric Riemannian Spaces With Applications to Dirichlet Series_, J. Indian Math. Soc. 20 (1956).**

Selberg's trace formula is retained as a solved analogue because the spectral operator is independently defined and the spectral/geometric identity is rigorous on a controlled test-function class. The disanalogy is load-bearing: prime geodesic data do not equal the Riemann prime-power/von-Mangoldt weights.

## 4. Adelic/noncommutative route

**A. Connes, _Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function_, Selecta Math. 5 (1999), arXiv:math/9811068.**

Connes gives a serious arithmetic trace framework in which critical zeros are interpreted through an absorption spectrum and the explicit formulas arise in an adelic/noncommutative setting. The paper explicitly leaves trace-formula validity as a load-bearing route to RH. `RH-SPEC-001` therefore does not treat the formal trace representation as already closed.

## 5. Semiclassical Hilbert–Polya route

**M. V. Berry and J. P. Keating, _The Riemann Zeros and Eigenvalue Asymptotics_, SIAM Review 41 (1999).**

The spectral statistics and counting asymptotics motivate an unknown Hermitian operator, and `H_cl=xp` is proposed as a semiclassical model. This is retained as candidate-generation context only. Exact operator domain, self-adjointness, arithmetic trace terms, complete multiplicity correspondence, and non-circular boundary data remain independent obligations.

## 6. Finite-field solved analogue

**P. Deligne, _La conjecture de Weil II_, Publ. Math. IHES 52 (1980).**

Primary proof source for the finite-field Riemann-Hypothesis analogue. The useful transfer pattern is: independently defined state space/operator + exact zeta correspondence + deep positivity/duality control. The missing classical analogue is itself part of the obstruction.

## 7. Real-zero deformation contrast

**B. Rodgers and T. Tao, _The de Bruijn-Newman constant is non-negative_, arXiv:1801.05914 (2018).**

This rigorous deformation lane is a contrastive near-solved context. RH is equivalent to the complementary inequality `Lambda <= 0`, while Rodgers–Tao prove `Lambda >= 0`. It is useful for identifying what a real-zero-preserving deformation controls, but it does not supply a self-adjoint arithmetic operator.

## Source-to-claim boundary

The packet supports only these context claims:

- exact RH statement and Weil sign criterion;
- existence of solved finite-field and Selberg trace analogues;
- existence and stated scope of Connes, Berry–Keating, and de Bruijn–Newman programmes;
- explicit disanalogies and proof obligations.

It does **not** claim that any listed route proves RH, that spectral resemblance implies an exact trace identity, or that an analogy supplies theorem authority.

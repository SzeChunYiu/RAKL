# Canonical problem contract

## Root statement

Let

`xi(s) = (1/2) s(s-1) pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2) zeta(s)`.

The Riemann Hypothesis is the exact statement that every nontrivial zero `rho` of `zeta(s)` satisfies

`Re(rho) = 1/2`.

Equivalently, if `Xi(t) = xi(1/2 + i t)`, every zero of `Xi(t)` is real.

No neighboring statement is silently treated as equivalent.

## Registered sufficient spectral bridge B1 — exact Hilbert–Polya realization

A construction is sufficient for RH if it supplies an independently defined self-adjoint operator `H` on an explicitly specified Hilbert space and proves that the complete relevant spectrum of `H`, with multiplicity and no missing/spurious terms, is exactly the multiset of ordinates `gamma` for nontrivial zeros `1/2 + i gamma` of `zeta`.

Self-adjointness alone is not enough; the exact all-zero correspondence is a separate proof obligation.

## Registered sufficient positivity bridge B2 — exact Weil-form factorization

Weil's explicit-formula criterion gives an RH-equivalent global sign condition on a registered admissible test-function class. A construction is sufficient for RH if it unconditionally proves, on that full exact class, that the RH-equivalent Weil quadratic functional is an exact sign-definite Hilbert-space norm or positive-operator quadratic form, with all prime-power, archimedean, null-space, moment, domain, and convergence terms accounted for, and with positivity proved independently of RH.

This is a sufficient bridge only when the factorization is independently derived. Rewriting the desired sign condition as a formal square without proving the transform/domain/positivity is not progress.

## Failure conditions

A candidate does **not** solve the root if it proves or exhibits only:

- finitely many critical-line zeros;
- the Riemann–von Mangoldt mean zero-counting asymptotic;
- GUE statistics, pair correlation, or another statistical resemblance;
- a formal trace identity without legitimate domain/convergence/distributional and correction-term control;
- an operator whose boundary condition, domain, kernel, determinant, or spectrum is fitted from the target zeros;
- a self-adjoint spectrum matching only a subset or asymptotic summary of the zeros;
- an absorption/resonance picture without the exact theorem needed to force the RH-equivalent sign or zero location;
- an RH theorem for a neighboring zeta/L-function without an audited implication to the classical Riemann zeta root;
- computation or numerical evidence without a proof.

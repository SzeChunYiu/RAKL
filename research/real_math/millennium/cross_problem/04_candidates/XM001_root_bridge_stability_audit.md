### Cheapest target falsifier

Let the transfer-eigenvalue ratio surrogate be

\[
r(a,L)=\exp(-a^2),\qquad a>0,
\]

independent of volume parameter `L`. Then

\[
0<r(a,L)<1,
\]

and the dimensionless log-gap is

\[
\delta(a,L)=-\log r(a,L)=a^2>0.
\]

It is positive for every lattice spacing and is uniform in `L`. But under the physical inverse-spacing normalization registered by `YM-S1`,

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{phys}}(a,L)=\frac{\delta(a,L)}{a}=a\to 0
\quad (a\to0).
\]

Therefore the weak inference

> “positive dimensionless gap for every finite lattice spacing, even uniformly in volume at each fixed spacing”

`=>`

> “positive physical continuum mass gap”

is false without a stronger quantitative bridge.

This says nothing about whether actual Yang–Mills transfer matrices can realize this sequence. It only proves that pointwise positivity is not logically sufficient.

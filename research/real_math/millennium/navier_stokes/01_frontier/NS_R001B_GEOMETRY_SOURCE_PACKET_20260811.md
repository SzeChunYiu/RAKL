## Primary geometric regularity anchors

- P. Constantin and C. Fefferman, *Direction of Vorticity and the Problem of Global Regularity for the Navier-Stokes Equations*, Indiana Univ. Math. J. 42 (1993), 775–789, DOI `10.1512/iumj.1993.42.42034`. Use: geometric coherence of vorticity direction can weaken the nonlocal vortex-stretching mechanism and imply regularity under explicit solution-side hypotheses. Non-use: it does not derive those hypotheses from arbitrary Leray-energy data.
- E. Miller, *A Regularity Criterion for the Navier-Stokes Equation Involving Only the Middle Eigenvalue of the Strain Tensor*, Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 235 (2020), 99–139, DOI `10.1007/s00205-019-01419-z`, together with the author's addendum DOI `10.1007/s00205-020-01527-1`. Use: strain eigenvalue geometry gives scale-critical blowup/regularity information. Historical boundary: the addendum records that a main middle-eigenvalue criterion had prior work by Neustupa and Penel; this packet makes no priority or novelty claim.
- E. Miller, *On the interaction of strain and vorticity for solutions of the Navier-Stokes equation*, arXiv:`2407.02691` (2024). Use: the exact strain formulation separates self-amplification, strain-vorticity interaction, and advection; it proves the identity `<-ΔS,ω⊗ω>=0`, global regularity for a strain-vorticity interaction model, and conditional criteria that quantify when the full equation is close to a regularizing or amplifying model. Non-use: these are not an a priori global bound from arbitrary finite-energy data.

## Why this changes the next discriminator

The parent A1 failure left several possibilities: geometry, pressure cancellation, frequency locality, or another exact identity. The primary sources above show that geometry is meaningful **for actual solutions**, but they also make clear that existing criteria are conditional. Therefore the next cheapest discriminator should not propose a new regularity theorem. It should test which coordinates are truly load-bearing by separating:

1. a smooth finite-energy divergence-free field with strong local positive strain-vorticity alignment but **no exact Navier-Stokes evolution**; and
2. an exact smooth Navier-Stokes linear flow with the same positive alignment but **no finite-energy/spatial-decay property**.

A route that cannot explain why these two calibrations are excluded for different reasons has not yet isolated the Clay-relevant mechanism.

# RAKL Core Principles

1. **Object before answer.** Define what is being studied before comparing claims.
2. **Projection before competition.** A source is first treated as a contextual projection on an object facet, not a whole-object rival.
3. **Context before contradiction.** Align population, scale, observation, assumptions, units, and intervention before declaring claims inconsistent.
4. **Atomic recursion.** Every unresolved transformation may become its own knowledge fiber.
5. **Context fiber before candidate generation.** After atomization, freeze the active atom's structural coordinates, equivalent formulations, solved/near-solved analogues, method-transfer assumptions, disanalogies and repair questions before proposing a solution candidate. A bibliography or analogy label is not sufficient.
6. **Analogies require a structural witness.** Cross-domain and everyday situations may expand the invention basis only when the common abstraction, source-to-target mapping, shared constraints, disanalogies and a falsifiable transfer obligation are explicit. Surface resemblance is rejected.
7. **Analogy proposes; mathematics/evidence disposes.** A human-life analogy, metaphor, game, engineering pattern or biological mechanism can suggest a candidate move but can never certify that move in the target domain.
8. **Research decisions are auditable.** Record material state transitions: current context, atomization result, alternatives considered, concise decision basis, action, evidence, result, uncertainty/residual and proposed next step. The trace is a reproducible decision record, not a raw private chain-of-thought transcript.
9. **Normalize before multiplying.** Map equivalent representations and terminology before expanding the global combination space.
10. **Compatibility before testing.** Prune mathematically, causally, observationally, or operationally impossible combinations before empirical search.
11. **Representation is not mechanism.** Predictive success may identify a useful effective law without identifying its microscopic cause.
12. **Mechanism requires ancestry.** A mechanistic claim must connect building blocks, interactions, coarse variables, effective terms, and downstream consequences.
13. **Formal invention must be externalized.** A certifying invented theory must be represented as typed symbols, equations/operators, mechanism structure, assumptions/regimes, falsifiers and lineage rather than remaining only natural-language reasoning.
14. **Residuals are information.** Failure should reopen the fiber dimensions capable of generating that residual.
15. **Failed candidates do not satisfy a positive goal.** When a lane has a frozen positive-goal contract, a failed candidate is retained evidence and a trigger for further invention; it is not successful project closure.
16. **Persistence never licenses fabrication.** A mandatory positive objective cannot justify invented evidence, target leakage, selective deletion, hidden multiplicity, or post-result threshold rescue.
17. **Discriminate rather than decorate.** Prefer experiments that make surviving hypotheses disagree.
18. **Known-answer and hidden-answer worlds first.** Validators and invention operators must succeed, fail, and return cannot-check in controlled or withheld-target worlds before native capability claims are trusted.
19. **Preserve negative history.** A refutation or null is evidence. A successor is a new versioned object.
20. **Exact over local where needed.** Audit bounds, linearisations, inversions, and proxy identities against the actual operating region.
21. **LLM proposes, evidence disposes.** The model can generate ideas, mathematical structures and syntheses but cannot promote them by confidence alone.
22. **Candidate identity before evaluation.** An invented formalism, its thresholds, predictions and falsifiers must be frozen before the evidence used to certify that candidate is exposed.
23. **Blind critique is different from reflection.** Same-context self-critique is useful but not independent review.
24. **Raw is not canonical.** Search discoveries and candidate mappings enter raw storage before promotion.
25. **Semantic novelty, not paper count.** Saturation measures retained new knowledge after deduplication.
26. **Knowledge saturation is not positive-goal closure.** Exhausting the current literature or model menu does not prove that a registered positive objective has been achieved or disproved.
27. **RAKL applies to RAKL.** Every method step, including the invention-operator basis, may be challenged by better practices under frozen meta-evaluation.
28. **Search the obstruction, not the vocabulary.** When solving a new problem, represent the load-bearing relational obstruction and ask whether an obstruction→transformation episode with the same structural skeleton exists anywhere in recorded knowledge. Lexical or embedding closeness alone is not a witness.
29. **SEARCH, then JUMP, then GLUE, then LIFT.** Reuse directly applicable transformations first; transport distant ones only with a structural mapping witness; compose partial transformations only with interface obligations; invent only after the earlier route families are explicitly exhausted or blocked in a bounded search.
30. **Repeated failure constrains invention.** A single failure does not justify a new tool. Repeated residual structure across distinct failures may specify what a missing transformation must preserve, break, expose or reduce, and that specification remains proposal-only until target validation.
31. **A shortcut changes search cost, not truth authority.** A successful semantic route can make a proof or discovery easier to find, but proof, evidence, novelty, mechanism and method-promotion authority remain with their existing validation gates.

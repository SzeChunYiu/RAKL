\section{The capstone question: recursive improvement under a protected evaluator epoch}
\label{sec:evaluator-governed-recursion}

Paper~III owns the general calculus of evidence-governed method evolution.  The capstone question here is narrower and empirical: \emph{can the assembled engine identify a real weakness, localize its cause, propose the smallest justified method change, and improve on fresh work while the evaluator that authorizes that comparison remains outside the candidate's control?}  This distinction prevents the systems paper from re-publishing the method-evolution calculus as a larger architecture diagram.

\paragraph{Evaluator epochs.}
For a scoped experiment we bind an evaluator epoch
\[
\mathcal E=
(h_{\mathrm{subject}},h_{\mathrm{eval}},h_{\mathrm{deps}},h_{\mathrm{env}},
 h_{\mathrm{bench}},h_{\mathrm{metric}},h_{\mathrm{resource}},C),
\]
where the hashes identify the exact research subject, evaluator source, dependencies and environment, benchmark artifacts, metric semantics and resource contract, and $C$ is the cutoff.  Within one epoch these objects are fixed.  Candidate outcomes may motivate a later evaluator change, but cannot be used to construct the outer evaluator that certifies that same candidate.

If evaluator policy itself must change, the current epoch closes.  The new evaluator is a higher-order method candidate: it requires separately frozen outer assurance, anchor replay and an explicit drift comparison before a new epoch begins.  Scores from two evaluator epochs are therefore not silently spliced together as though the ruler had remained unchanged.  A display-name change is not an independent evaluator; the recursive controller's research successor binds evaluator source, dependency, metric, benchmark and environment identities so naming-only changes fail closed.

\paragraph{Diagnosis precedes mutation.}
The same fail-closed rule applies before self-modification.  When a residual is compatible with multiple mutation layers, the system does not mutate all plausible layers and call the surviving variant ``learning.''  It consumes a decision-bearing discriminator receipt that binds the before/after diagnosis states, selected discriminator, evidence epoch and cost.  The discriminator-selection algorithm itself remains owned by the existing diagnosis/value-of-information lane; the capstone controller consumes that receipt rather than creating a second, competing diagnosis policy.

\paragraph{Known-world control result.}
A frozen 4,096-world mechanical experiment was executed before any external-agent claim.  In the diagnosis sub-study, the registered decision-value selector chose exactly the same \texttt{surface} probe as both the fixed-surface parent and the information-gain-per-cost parent.  All three consequently returned the same vector: correct-repair rate $0.51245$, wrong-layer and harmful-mutation rate $0.48755$, and total cost $0.35$ per world.  The diagnosis component therefore terminates \texttt{SIMPLER\_PARENT\_SUFFICIENT}; no distinct value-of-information mechanic is claimed from this world.

The contextual-credit sub-study gives a different, scoped result.  Global operator credit selected \texttt{local\_patch} in every fresh context, making a harmful repair in one of three fresh contexts and incurring mean oracle regret $0.18333$.  Credit transported only across a pre-registered same-family witness selected the fresh oracle operator in all three contexts, with harmful-repair rate and regret both zero.  Because the diagnosis component is matched by simpler parents, the \emph{overall} frozen terminal remains \texttt{SIMPLER\_PARENT\_SUFFICIENT}; the contextual-credit result is retained as a component-level known-world mechanic result rather than used to upgrade the capstone claim.

\paragraph{What remains external.}
The publication-grade capstone still requires a fresh external epoch: compare the incumbent against strong internal and external research-agent parents; freeze one measured weakness; diagnose it; assimilate or invent the smallest justified challenger; then compare incumbent, challenger and winning parent on disjoint fresh tasks under the unchanged evaluator epoch.  \texttt{EXTERNAL\_PARENT\_WINS}, \texttt{META\_OVERFIT}, \texttt{BENCHMARK\_INVALID}, \texttt{CANNOT\_CHECK} and a supported fresh-assimilation result are all first-class terminals.  Until that experiment exists, this section establishes a governed experimental object and scoped control evidence, not autonomous scientific superiority or globally optimal self-improvement.

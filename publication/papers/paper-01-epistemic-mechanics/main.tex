\begin{abstract}
Scientific reasoning systems may be operated by humans, deterministic algorithms, theorem provers, learned models, large language models (LLMs), or hybrids. The executor does not determine which outputs are licensed to change a scientific record. We formalize that boundary in RAKL and its reference substrate Orion. Claims are indexed by context, evidence, uncertainty and provenance; compatibility uses explicit alignments; scientific authority is multi-coordinate; and proposal, routing, workspace and learning transitions are non-sovereign unless a separate certificate authorizes a canonical update. We prove higher-order gluing and projection-sufficiency obstructions. A repaired 28-case, 14-family development audit identifies a ten-coordinate authority basis that separates the registered decision twins, with each coordinate individually necessary on the panel; a richer truth-maintenance/provenance/revision parent closes part of the gap, so no generic rule-engine expressiveness claim is made. Six frozen known-world benefit cells then test projection, composition, gluing, authority, navigation and bounded stopping. These are deterministic mechanism experiments, not LLM-accuracy measurements. A source-monitoring repetition failure is preserved and followed by an unchanged-threshold source-identity repair that passes its hostile and near-miss controls; a separate downstream saturation solve-enablement null remains preserved and is diagnosed as underpowered rather than relabeled. LLMs are optional proposal or representation adapters under the same rules as human and symbolic executors. The contribution is an executor-independent account of scientific justification inside a recursively learning research substrate, not a claim of global completeness or universal empirical superiority.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Successful outcomes need provenance of the information state that produced them}

Agent evaluation can be confounded even when the final answer is correct. Luo and Peng distinguish ordinary outcome accuracy from \emph{success provenance}: whether the evaluated information state actually supported the success or whether the agent acquired a target value through an evaluation surface that was not intended to supply it \cite{luo2026success}. Their matched CLEAN/GOLD/SHAM interventions show why exposure detection alone is insufficient when success causally tracks acquired target information.

This suggests an additional audit for scientific-agent comparisons. Evaluation receipts should distinguish evidence legitimately available under the protocol from target information acquired through unintended benchmark, tool, memory or environment channels. A high score under contaminated information state cannot be interpreted as evidence that the registered scientific reasoning path succeeded. RAKL's provenance and availability-oracle machinery already represent the required ingredients; the benchmark supplies a closer evaluation precedent and makes the causal intervention explicit. In a matched workflow trial, a suspected information-state leak should therefore trigger a sham-substitution or equivalent intervention where feasible rather than being treated as a cosmetic data-cleaning concern.

\begin{abstract}
Scientific transfer has two separable problems: constructing a useful representation and deciding whether a proposed mapping is licensed. We study the second problem. RAKL's structural contract binds a quantity of interest, role and relation mappings, preserved invariants, forbidden losses, target boundaries, evidence and uncertainty, and returns LICENSED, REJECTED or CANNOT_CHECK without scalar compensation. Against causal transportability as a formal parent, we show that importing completeness-style refusal into an open witness setting can create false impossibility certificates; an adapted typed refusal is correct on the registered known-world arms. We then use three failed instruments to derive a falsifiability battery for transfer studies. An external third-party-labelled analogy experiment passes that battery but yields a negative for the registered deterministic reducer, localizing the remaining gap to representation acquisition rather than to the licence itself. The framework is executor-independent: source and target structures may be proposed by humans, symbolic algorithms, theorem provers, LLMs or hybrids, while the same downstream obligations govern all of them. Progressive abstraction and typed morphism registries are prospective acquisition extensions, not evidence already earned by this paper.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code, materials and AI-use disclosure}
A public research artifact accompanies this paper. Frozen protocols, receipts, audit batteries and machine-readable negative results are retained alongside the manuscript so that publication prose cannot silently replace missing evidence; in particular, every instrument refutation reported here (the non-falsifiable six-family gate, the serialization-fidelity reading of the controlled-witness instrument, and the template-inversion terminal of the prose instrument) is preserved as a first-class artifact rather than summarized away. Language models were used as research, coding and drafting tools; they are not authors. Same-context analysis, including the audit batteries, is explicitly distinguished from independent review, and no external judgement is represented as completed until a hash-bound response, adjudication and provenance audit exist. Author identity, affiliation and corresponding email are given on the title page. Funding, competing-interest, acknowledgement and licensing declarations remain author-supplied submission metadata and are not inferred by the repository.

\section{Verified transformation geometry as a proposal-only search projection}
\label{sec:vtg}

The assurance architecture above determines when a mathematical claim may change durable authority. It intentionally does not identify proof search with theorem truth. This section sharpens that separation for a new, still-unresolved research question: whether verifier-defined mathematical reachability admits a useful \emph{local} geometry at all.

\subsection{Exact transition semantics, discovered map and solution region}
Fix a formal target $P$, assumptions $\Gamma$, a registered logic/checker $L$, an operator basis $\Omega$ and resource budget $B$. Let
\[
\Sigma_{P,\Gamma,L}(x,T,y)\in\{\PASS,\FAIL,\CC\}
\]
be the exact transition semantics: whether applying the typed transformation $T$ to state $x$ yields $y$ under the registered verifier. This semantic relation is distinct from the finite materialized subcomplex $\mathcal C^+_{P,t}$ containing only states and transitions actually discovered and replay-verified by time $t$.

The operational map is therefore a partial-belief object
\[
\mathcal B_{P,t}=(K^+_t,K^-_t,K^?_t,U_t,\Gamma_{\mathrm{cov},t}),
\]
where $K^+$ are verified transitions, $K^-$ are scoped refutations or blockers, $K^?$ are candidate/unverified transitions, $U$ represents explicitly unknown/unexplored regions and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{cov}}$ records the coverage contract. The binding rule is
\[
\boxed{\text{unknown transition}\;\neq\;\text{blocked transition}.}
\]
Failure to find a route in an incomplete $\mathcal B_{P,t}$ is therefore a search result, not a mathematical impossibility result.

The verified solution region is defined independently of any geometry:
\[
\mathcal V_P=\{x:\operatorname{OriginalSemanticsCheck}(x,P,\Gamma,L)=\PASS\}.
\]
No distance, embedding, field, search frequency or model confidence can put a state into $\mathcal V_P$.

\subsection{Directed higher-order structure and path quotienting}
Mathematical transformations are generally directed, and proofs are generally not single linear paths. A solver may follow a chronological search trajectory
\[
s_0\to s_1\to\cdots\to s_n,
\]
while the final proof certificate is a dependency DAG, proof term or typed hypergraph. The implementation now treats these as separate objects: \texttt{TaskEpisode} preserves chronology, while \texttt{SolutionAssemblyReceipt} can become \path{READY_FOR_EXTERNAL_AUTHORITY_GATE} only after exact DAG hashing, root-dependency closure, verified dependencies, a verified root and an assembly-verifier pass. The assembly receipt itself still grants no theorem authority.

State canonicalization also leaves a second form of redundancy. When two transformations are independently replayable and commute in a registered context, the histories $T_1;T_2$ and $T_2;T_1$ need not be searched as unrelated proofs. Orion therefore admits a verifier-witnessed path-equivalence relation and a partial-order trace as proposal/search views. This is intentionally stronger than merely embedding similar proof traces near one another: a non-trivial path-equivalence witness requires explicit conditions and verifier identities.

\subsection{Goal-relative solvability geometry}
A candidate solvability geometry is a derived object
\[
\mathcal G_{P,t,B}=\operatorname{Geom}(\mathcal B_{P,t},\Omega,R,B,Q),
\]
indexed by target/specification, operator basis, representation chart $R$, map revision, budget and quantity of interest. Reachability is normally asymmetric, so the natural primitive is a directed cost-to-go, quasimetric, vector or set-valued bound rather than an assumed Euclidean distance. In particular,
\[
d_{\Omega,R,B}(x,\mathcal V_P)=\infty
\]
may mean only ``unreachable under the current operator basis/chart/budget''; it is not mathematical non-existence.

The candidate geometry may influence routing only. Let $\pi_{\mathrm{math}}$ denote the mathematical-authority projection and let $G$ be any update that changes only map estimates, coordinates, distances, field values or navigation conductance. The implementation contract is
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{math}}(G(x))=\pi_{\mathrm{math}}(x).
\]
Thus a monotonically decreasing energy, short geometric route or highly reinforced proof path remains proposal-level until the exact proof/specification/trust gates pass.

\begin{proposition}[Unknown-map and geometric noninterference]
Suppose (i) the operational-map router traverses only verifier-bound $K^+$ edges when reporting a verified route, (ii) any incomplete coverage or candidate/unknown edge prevents a no-route result from being interpreted as scoped complete-map closure, and (iii) geometry/dynamics have no write access to mathematical authority. Then neither an undiscovered valid transition nor an erroneous distance estimate can by itself promote or refute the target theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An undiscovered valid transition lies outside the materialized verified subcomplex, so by (ii) its absence cannot produce the complete-map no-route verdict. A geometry can change only the order in which candidate transitions are inspected; by (iii) it cannot change theorem authority. Any route presented as verified consists only of transitions satisfying (i), and the final theorem still passes the independent solution-assembly and mathematical-authority gates. Hence map incompleteness and geometry error can cause search cost or search failure, but not direct theorem promotion or refutation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fieldability is closed-loop, not local correlation}
A useful geometry must be evaluated as a policy substrate, not by the correlation of one-step scores alone. The registered \texttt{FieldabilityProfile} therefore separates construction cost, reuse break-even, invalidation/staleness, local action alignment, strict-greedy completion, bounded-branch completion, route stretch, false-descent/trap rate, verifier calls and out-of-distribution failure.

The distinction is necessary because a locally informative score can still induce a globally incomplete greedy policy. Conversely, a geometry that is not greedily navigable may still have substantial value if it sharply reduces a bounded frontier. We therefore propose the local-navigability curve
\[
N(k,B)=\bigl(\text{verified success},\text{route stretch},\text{states expanded},\text{verifier cost}\bigr),
\]
where $k$ is the amount of local branching/information permitted and $B$ is the resource ceiling.

\subsection{Phase-0/1 falsification experiment}
The next mathematical epistemic cut is frozen as:
\[
\boxed{\text{Does verifier-defined mathematical reachability admit a nontrivially local navigable geometry?}}
\]
The first experiment must not use an LLM as the solver. A finite Lean environment is split into: (a) an evaluator-only gold transition/proof universe; (b) a solver-visible partial map with hidden gold routes and intermediate labels removed; (c) a geometry-construction/training split; and (d) held-out theorem families. Every state and transition is bound to the exact Lean environment, theorem/specification hash, local context and verifier result.

The registered controls are ordinary graph search/best-first, a gold-distance oracle available only to the evaluator, simple structural heuristics and a learned proof-progress baseline where reproducibly available. Candidate geometries are introduced one family at a time---for example directed/quasimetric or reachability representations, followed by multi-chart/quotient-aware forms only if simpler candidates leave a residual. Each geometry is tested under strict greedy, bounded-local branching and geometry-guided best-first policies. Geometry construction and verifier cost are included in the resource account.

The lane is declared unsupported in the registered scope if, after hidden-route leakage controls and total construction cost, no candidate geometry improves verified proof success or search cost over strong controls on held-out theorem families. A narrower positive outcome is allowed: for example, \path{LOCALLY_INFORMATIVE_NOT_GREEDILY_NAVIGABLE} or \path{ATLAS_NAVIGABILITY_SUPPORTED_GLOBAL_GEOMETRY_UNSUPPORTED}. Only after a positive Phase-1 signal would flow, diffusion, path-integral or conductance dynamics become justified experiments.

\paragraph{Relationship to the broader Orion mechanics.}
Verified Transformation Geometry is the mathematical instantiation of the broader proposal-only Verified Solver Compilation layer. If navigation fails, Mechanic Differential Diagnosis asks whether the obstruction belongs to specification, map coverage, representation/portal, scale, metric, dynamics, operator basis, verifier, interface, model/tool floor or compilation cost. A representation or method change is then requested by its desired transformation effect and must return through the same specification, preservation and proof-assurance gates described above. This preserves the design goal of aggressive search with conservative authority.

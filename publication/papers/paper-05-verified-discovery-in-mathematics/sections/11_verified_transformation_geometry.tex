\section{Verified transformation geometry as a proposal-only search projection}
\label{sec:vtg}

The assurance architecture above determines when a mathematical claim may change durable authority. It intentionally does not identify proof search with theorem truth. This section sharpens that separation for a new, still-unresolved research question: whether verifier-defined mathematical reachability admits a useful \emph{local} geometry at all. The formulation below distinguishes exact operational semantics, partial knowledge of that semantics, path equivalence, representation change, intrinsic path cost, and budget-conditioned control. Those distinctions are required before geometric language can carry mathematical meaning.

\subsection{Frozen subject and operational-state sufficiency}
Fix a formal target $P$, assumptions $\Gamma$, a registered logic/checker $L$, an exact environment/version $E$, an operator basis $\Omega$, an active representation $R$, a root quantity of interest $Q$, and a registered admissibility/cost profile $A$. Write the frozen solver subject as
\[
\Theta=(P,\Gamma,L,E,\Omega,R,Q,A).
\]
Any change to a load-bearing coordinate defines a new subject unless an explicit transport certificate licenses reuse.

A state geometry is well defined only if the operational state records all future-relevant information. Let $h_1$ and $h_2$ be two execution histories that are encoded as the same state $x$. The encoding is \emph{operationally sufficient} when, under frozen $\Theta$, the legal successor relation and transition-cost law from $x$ are independent of whether $x$ was reached through $h_1$ or $h_2$. If proof-state metadata, mutable environment, caches, accumulated assumptions, solver memory or another history-dependent coordinate changes future legal moves, that coordinate must either be included in the operational state or retained in an explicitly separate control/resource state. Without this condition, a purported quantity $d(x,y)$ is history dependent and therefore not a function of states.

\subsection{Exact transition semantics, discovered map and solution region}
Let
\[
\Sigma_{\Theta}(x,T,y)\in\{\PASS,\FAIL,\CC\}
\]
be the exact transition semantics: whether applying the typed transformation $T$ to state $x$ yields $y$ under the registered verifier. This semantic relation is distinct from the finite materialized subcomplex $\mathcal C^+_{\Theta,t}$ containing only states and transitions actually discovered and replay-verified by time $t$.

The operational map is therefore a partial-belief object
\[
\mathcal B_{\Theta,t}=(K^+_t,K^-_t,K^?_t,U_t,\Gamma_{\mathrm{cov},t}),
\]
where $K^+$ are materialized verified transitions, $K^-$ are scoped refutations or blockers, $K^?$ are candidate/unverified transitions, $U$ represents explicitly unknown/unexplored regions and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{cov}}$ records the coverage contract. The binding rule is
\[
\boxed{\text{unknown transition}\;\neq\;\text{blocked transition}.}
\]
Failure to find a route in an incomplete $\mathcal B_{\Theta,t}$ is therefore a search result, not a mathematical impossibility result.

Even a certified complete \emph{registered operational map} supports only a scoped statement: no route exists under the bound subject, chart and operator basis. It does not by itself establish theorem falsity, mathematical non-existence, or even proof-system unprovability. Lifting such a result to unprovability would require a separate completeness theorem connecting the registered operator basis to the proof calculus; theorem refutation requires ordinary mathematical evidence such as a proof of negation or countermodel.

The verified solution region is defined independently of any geometry:
\[
\mathcal V_P=\{x:\operatorname{OriginalSemanticsCheck}(x,P,\Gamma,L)=\PASS\}.
\]
No distance, embedding, field, search frequency or model confidence can put a state into $\mathcal V_P$.

\subsection{Verified paths, higher-order proof structure and congruent path quotienting}
Let $\operatorname{Path}(\Theta)$ denote the free category generated by materialized verified elementary transitions: states are objects, verified finite paths are morphisms, empty paths are identities and path concatenation is composition. This microscopic object is directed and symbolic; no useful geometry is assumed.

Proof discovery and proof certification remain distinct. A solver may follow a chronological trajectory
\[
s_0\to s_1\to\cdots\to s_n,
\]
while the final proof certificate is a dependency DAG, proof term or typed hypergraph. \texttt{TaskEpisode} preserves chronology; the proof DAG/certificate preserves the mathematical dependency object. A solution-assembly receipt is proposal/assurance plumbing and cannot itself grant theorem authority.

State canonicalization also leaves path redundancy. A path equivalence relation $\sim$ is safe for quotienting only when it is an endpoint-preserving congruence:
\[
\gamma\sim\delta\Longrightarrow s(\gamma)=s(\delta),\quad t(\gamma)=t(\delta),
\]
and, whenever the compositions are defined,
\[
\gamma\sim\delta\Longrightarrow \alpha\gamma\beta\sim\alpha\delta\beta.
\]
Thus equivalence is not merely similarity of traces. For concurrency reduction, Orion permits an adjacent exchange
\[
uabv\sim ubav
\]
only when a scoped verifier-bound witness establishes that $a$ and $b$ are independent/commuting transformations in the relevant context. Absence of a declared dependency is not evidence of independence. The quotient $\operatorname{Path}(\Theta)/{\sim}$ therefore removes only witnessed redundancy.

Path equivalence is semantic, not automatically economic: equivalent paths can have different cache behavior, wall time or verification cost. A search quotient must retain the relevant cost frontier unless cost invariance is separately established.

\subsection{Representations, portals and navigation quotients}
The solver representation system is more general than a differential-geometric atlas. Let $\mathsf{Rep}_P$ be a transformation network/category whose objects are problem representations and whose arrows are certified transformations such as exact equivalences, one-way reductions, quotients, bounded approximations, relaxations and dualities. The exact invertible arrows form the atlas/groupoid-like portion; irreversible reductions should not be called coordinate changes without qualification.

A target-conditioned semantic quotient can preserve the root QoI while still changing reachability. Therefore a quotient used for navigation requires a separate \emph{navigation quotient contract}. For a quotient $q:X\to\bar X$, an exact reachability-preserving claim requires at least target-label preservation and suitable two-way transition conditions. A forward simulation may be sufficient for a sound over-approximation; an abstract path presented as executable requires a route-lifting/backward condition or an explicit spurious-route/refinement outcome. If the quotient is used to estimate route cost, the distortion relation between abstract and concrete costs must also be stated or left unidentified.

\subsection{Hard admissibility and intrinsic path-cost geometry}
A mathematical route first passes non-compensatory admissibility. Let
\[
\Lambda(e)\in\{\PASS,\FAIL,\CC\}
\]
encode hard requirements such as specification alignment, licensed assumptions, valid representation portals, trusted verifier semantics and root-scope preservation. Cheap computation cannot compensate for $\Lambda(e)=\FAIL$.

For admissible transitions, path cost lives in a registered algebra $A$ rather than an assumed universal scalar. Different coordinates may compose differently: compute/time may be additive, peak memory may use a maximum, assumptions naturally compose by set union, trust may use a worst-case/meet operation, and evidence carries provenance rather than a generic numeric sum. When the chosen carrier and composition support an enriched/generalized metric construction, define the intrinsic operator/representation-relative path value
\[
D_{\Theta}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma:x\rightsquigarrow y} C_A(\gamma).
\]
If the task does not justify one scalar/ordered algebra, Orion retains a Pareto or set-valued reachability frontier and does not promote it to a scalar distance by convention.

Crucially, the resource budget is not part of the intrinsic geometric axioms. Budget $B$ defines feasible reachability/control:
\[
x\Rightarrow_B y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\gamma:x\rightsquigarrow y\text{ with }C_A(\gamma)\preceq B.
\]
A budget-truncated distance can violate triangle inequality: two six-unit legs can each be feasible under a ten-unit cutoff while their twelve-unit composition is mapped to infinity. Goal-relative $V_{P,B}$ is therefore a control/value object above the intrinsic path geometry, not automatically a metric.

\subsection{Geometry certification classes and local navigability}
A candidate routing geometry is a derived, version-bound artifact
\[
\mathcal G_{\Theta,t}=\operatorname{Geom}(\mathcal B_{\Theta,t}),
\]
bound to the specification/QoI, verifier/environment subject, operator basis, representation, map revision, cost algebra and construction version. It must also declare the strongest certification class actually established, for example:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\texttt{EXACT\_COST\_TO\_GO},\quad
\texttt{ADMISSIBLE\_LOWER\_BOUND},\quad
\texttt{CONSISTENT\_HEURISTIC},\\
\texttt{EMPIRICAL\_RANKER},\quad
\texttt{UNCERTIFIED}.
\end{gathered}
\]
An empirical ranker can prioritize search but cannot inherit optimality/completeness claims that require an admissible or consistent heuristic.

The strongest local-navigation statement can be made theorem-like without assuming Euclidean geometry. Let $W$ be well founded and let $h:X\to W$. A local policy $\pi$ is \emph{descent-complete} on a registered solvable set $S$ when every non-goal $x\in S$ has a verified legal successor $\pi(x)$ with
\[
h(\pi(x))\prec h(x),
\]
and every minimal reachable $h$-state in $S$ is a verified goal. Then repeated application of $\pi$ reaches a goal in finitely many steps, because an infinite strictly descending chain in $W$ is impossible. For real-valued scores on infinite spaces, strict descent alone is not sufficient; a well-founded rank, uniform/proper progress condition or another termination certificate is required.

This separates three progressively stronger notions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{local congruence}: lower score tends to rank useful legal successors higher;
    \item \emph{closed-loop navigability}: a local/bounded-local policy reaches verified goals on the registered class;
    \item \emph{certified descent}: a well-founded progress argument proves termination for the registered class.
\end{enumerate}
The Phase-0/1 programme initially tests the first two; it does not presume the third exists for general mathematics.

\subsection{Monotonicity and authority noninterference}
Two simple monotonicity facts delimit what a map failure can mean. Under a frozen subject with unchanged edge semantics/costs, expanding the materialized verified map cannot remove an already verified route or worsen the best known route frontier. Likewise, if $\Omega\subseteq\Omega'$ and old operators retain their semantics, the reachable set under $\Omega'$ contains the reachable set under $\Omega$. Consequently, no-route under the current operator basis cannot imply no-route under a future enlarged basis.

Let $\pi_{\mathrm{math}}$ denote mathematical authority and let $G$ be any update that changes only map estimates, quotient views, geometry, field values, search conductance or route priority. The implementation contract remains
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{math}}(G(x))=\pi_{\mathrm{math}}(x).
\]
Thus a monotonically decreasing energy, short geometric route or highly reinforced proof path remains proposal-level until the exact proof/specification/trust gates pass.

\begin{proposition}[Map, geometry and quotient noninterference]
Suppose (i) a route is called verified only when every materialized transition on it is replay-verified under the frozen subject, (ii) incomplete map coverage cannot produce an impossibility or unprovability conclusion, (iii) path/representation quotients grant no authority and any route lifted from a quotient is revalidated in the original semantics, and (iv) geometry/dynamics have no write access to mathematical authority. Then map incompleteness, erroneous geometry, or a spurious abstract route can increase search cost or cause search failure, but cannot by itself promote or refute the target theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By (i), any route admitted as operationally verified has a concrete verifier-replayable representative. By (ii), missing roads cannot be converted into a negative theorem statement. By (iii), quotient membership or abstract reachability is not accepted as a concrete proof: a spurious route fails lifting/revalidation. Finally, (iv) blocks any direct promotion from routing state. Hence all three failure modes remain inside the search/control projection until an ordinary mathematical assurance gate is satisfied.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fieldability is closed-loop, not local correlation}
A useful geometry must be evaluated as a policy substrate, not by the correlation of one-step scores alone. The registered \texttt{FieldabilityProfile} therefore separates construction cost, reuse break-even, invalidation/staleness, local action alignment, strict-greedy completion, bounded-branch completion, route stretch, false-descent/trap rate, verifier calls and out-of-distribution failure.

The distinction is necessary because a locally informative score can still induce a globally incomplete greedy policy. Conversely, a geometry that is not greedily navigable may still have substantial value if it sharply reduces a bounded frontier. We therefore propose the local-navigability curve
\[
N(k,B)=\bigl(\text{verified success},\text{route stretch},\text{states expanded},\text{verifier cost}\bigr),
\]
where $k$ is the amount of local branching/information permitted and $B$ is the resource ceiling used by the policy evaluation.

\subsection{Phase-0/1 falsification experiment}
The next mathematical epistemic cut is frozen as:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.91\linewidth}{\centering
\textbf{Next mathematical epistemic cut:} Does verifier-defined mathematical reachability admit a nontrivially local navigable geometry?}}
\end{center}
The first experiment must not use an LLM as the solver. A finite Lean environment is split into: (a) an evaluator-only gold transition/proof universe; (b) a solver-visible partial map with hidden gold routes and intermediate labels removed; (c) a geometry-construction/training split; and (d) held-out theorem families. Every operational state is required to bind the future-relevant Lean environment/proof-state components needed for the registered transition semantics.

The registered controls are ordinary graph search/best-first, a gold-distance oracle available only to the evaluator, simple structural heuristics and a learned proof-progress baseline where reproducibly available. Candidate geometries are introduced one family at a time---for example directed/quasimetric or reachability representations, followed by multi-representation/quotient-aware forms only if simpler candidates leave a residual. Each geometry is tested under strict greedy, bounded-local branching and geometry-guided best-first policies. Geometry construction, abstraction/refinement, independence checking and verifier cost are included in the resource account.

A semantic quotient used for acceleration must separately report whether it is an exact navigation quotient or an over-approximation requiring route lifting/refinement. Gold routes, gold distance labels and future map edges remain evaluator-only. The lane is declared unsupported in the registered scope if, after hidden-route leakage controls and total construction cost, no candidate geometry improves verified proof success or search cost over strong controls on held-out theorem families. Narrower outcomes are allowed, for example \path{LOCALLY_INFORMATIVE_NOT_GREEDILY_NAVIGABLE}, \path{ATLAS_NAVIGABILITY_SUPPORTED_GLOBAL_GEOMETRY_UNSUPPORTED}, or \path{NO_SAFE_NAVIGATION_QUOTIENT_AT_TESTED_SCALE}. Only after a positive Phase-1 signal would flow, diffusion, path-integral or conductance dynamics become justified experiments.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The Phase-0/1 theory is deterministic. If later operators are stochastic or adversarial, the transition substrate must be upgraded to an appropriate stochastic kernel, MDP or game semantics; a deterministic edge geometry must not silently absorb stochasticity. Likewise, on infinite state spaces an infimum path value need not be attained and a geodesic need not exist. The word \emph{geometry} is therefore used broadly; metric, quasimetric, geodesic, manifold or fibred-category properties are claimed only when their respective axioms are separately established.

\paragraph{Relationship to the broader Orion mechanics.}
Verified Transformation Geometry is the mathematical instantiation of the broader proposal-only Verified Solver Compilation layer. If navigation fails, Mechanic Differential Diagnosis asks whether the obstruction belongs to specification, state sufficiency, map coverage, navigation quotient, representation/portal, scale, metric, dynamics, operator basis, verifier, interface, model/tool floor or compilation cost. A representation or method change is then requested by its desired transformation effect and must return through the same specification, preservation and proof-assurance gates described above. This preserves the design goal of aggressive search with conservative authority.

# Round 043 Citation Assimilation and Intellectual Lineage

Status: v2 paper research artifact.  
Rule: references are retained because they constrain, motivate or narrow a specific RAKL mechanism; bibliography size is not a target metric.

## 1. Scientific models are instruments to criticize, not final truth objects

- **Box (1976), _Science and Statistics_.** Absorbed mechanism: iterative model building, criticism and revision. RAKL implication: `model_criticism.py` is a first-class child operator; a model that passes a frozen probe family is only adequate for that scope.
- **Gelman, Meng & Stern (1996), posterior predictive assessment.** Absorbed mechanism: compare observed discrepancy statistics with predictive replicates. RAKL implication: generative adequacy probes are explicit and preregistered.
- **Breiman (2001), _Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures_.** Novelty correction: predictive/data-algorithmic modelling and explanatory data models are established competing traditions. RAKL does not conflate them.
- **Shmueli (2010), _To Explain or to Predict?_** Absorbed distinction: explanatory and predictive goals require different evidence and evaluation. RAKL maps this onto separate representation/mechanism/prediction authority axes.
- **Machamer, Darden & Craver (2000), _Thinking about Mechanisms_.** Prior art for explicit mechanism structure in terms of organized entities/activities. RAKL's mechanism ancestry is an operational evidence discipline, not a claim to invent mechanism-based explanation.

## 2. Negative history, assumptions and belief revision have deep prior art

- **Doyle (1979), Truth Maintenance System.** Prior art for recording reasons for beliefs and revising them when contradictions appear.
- **de Kleer (1986), Assumption-based TMS.** Prior art for assumption sets, inconsistent alternatives, context switching and simultaneous candidate solutions.

RAKL therefore does not claim novelty for belief revision or assumption-labelled alternatives. Its narrower contribution is to bind these ideas to scientific evidence lineage, multi-axis authority, immutable research failures and protected canonical updates.

## 3. Partial identification and assumption sensitivity

- **Manski (1990), nonparametric treatment-effect bounds.** Prior art for reporting identified sets rather than forcing point identification.
- **Cinelli & Hazlett (2020), sensitivity analysis.** Absorbed mechanism: quantify how conclusions change under plausible omitted-assumption worlds rather than simply state that assumptions may be wrong.

RAKL implication: `PARTIALLY_IDENTIFIED` is a legitimate terminal scientific state; `assumption_sensitivity.py` evaluates preregistered perturbation envelopes without claiming the perturbations themselves are true.

## 4. Provenance and research-object packaging

- **W3C PROV-DM (2013).** Prior art for entities, activities, agents, derivation and provenance constraints.
- **Wilkinson et al. (2016), FAIR principles.** Prior art for findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable research objects.
- **Soiland-Reyes et al. (2022), RO-Crate.** Prior art for structured, machine-readable packages of research artefacts, metadata, provenance and relationships.
- **Kuhn et al. (2018), nanopublications.** Prior art for atomic provenance-aware scientific statements.

RAKL implication: atomic provenance and research-object packaging are not standalone novelty. RAKL adds contextual scientific-authority semantics, negative-history governance, evidence-lineage-aware saturation and target-conditioned context compilation.

## 5. Analogy and cross-domain transfer

- **Gentner (1983), structure mapping.** Prior art for relationally structured analogy rather than superficial feature overlap.
- **Holyoak & Thagard (1989), constraint-satisfaction analogy.** Prior art for mappings jointly constrained by structural, semantic and pragmatic considerations.

RAKL implication: the `JUMP` channel and similarity witnesses are discovery mechanisms only. Explicit mappings must state preserved and non-preserved coordinates; analogical fit cannot mint target-domain scientific authority.

## 6. Active inquiry and experimental information

- **MacKay (1992), information-based active data selection.** Prior art for selecting observations by expected information value and for the warning that active-learning criteria rely on the assumed hypothesis space.
- **Dwork et al. (2015), reusable holdout.** Prior art for adaptive reuse of evaluation data creating validity risk.

RAKL implication: action selection may optimize information/separation per cost only when its assumptions are defensible; fresh assurance is treated as a consumable protected resource.

## 7. Metacognition, productive failure and self-regulated learning

- **Zimmerman (2002), self-regulated learning.** Absorbed high-level cycle: planning/forethought, monitoring/control and reflection are distinct functional operations.
- **Kapur (2008), productive failure.** Prior art for failure that improves later representations/transfer rather than simply being wasted attempts.
- **Pronin, Lin & Ross (2002), bias blind spot.** Motivation for not trusting self-description as independent diagnosis.
- **Rozenblit & Keil (2002), illusion of explanatory depth.** Motivation for explanation-reconstruction tests rather than confidence alone.
- **Lord, Lepper & Preston (1984), consider-the-opposite.** Prior art for explicit counter-hypothesis generation as a debiasing intervention.

RAKL implication: psychology terms are translated into executable control contracts: confidence calibration, explanation reconstruction, countermodels, strategy switching, help-seeking, learning progress, anti-rumination and transfer.

## 8. Memory, compression and context are prior engineering domains

The v1 paper already cites MemGPT, RAPTOR, LLMLingua and RECOMP. These establish virtual/context memory, hierarchical retrieval and compression as existing mechanisms. Round 043 therefore sharpens the RAKL distinction:

```text
scientific projection != semantic identity collapse != storage compression != prompt selection
```

The canonical archive is not replaced by an embedding or compressed latent state. Derived views remain source-pinned transformations with explicit loss/reconstruction metadata.

## 9. AI-scientist and self-evolution systems already cover broad automation

The existing v1 citations to Co-Scientist, Robin, AI Scientist-v2, Kosmos, SciAgents, PaperQA2, Darwin Godel Machine, EvoSkill, SkillFoundry and EvoAgentBench establish that multi-agent scientific workflows, long-horizon world state, end-to-end automation, skill libraries and agent self-modification are prior art.

RAKL's paper must therefore continue to lead with the narrower integrated claim:

> RAKL is a candidate scientific transition discipline in which contextual projection, typed relations, non-escalating authority, evidence lineage, immutable negative history, bounded epistemic context and protected method evolution jointly govern what an LLM-mediated research process may write into canonical scientific state.

## 10. Visual knowledge-map analogy

Obsidian-like graph navigation is useful UI prior art but not epistemic prior art for RAKL. A note graph links documents. RAKL's atlas links typed scientific objects under contexts, evidence, assumptions, authority and failure history.

Potential RAKL Atlas UI:

```text
global atlas
local target graph / support corridor
backlinks / incoming evidence ancestry
time slider for lattice growth
edge filters by relation type
node filters by authority/state/context
negative-history overlay
epistemic-cut overlay
active-prompt/token overlay
click-through to exact source span and raw payload
```

This is a product-design opportunity, not a novelty claim for graph visualization.

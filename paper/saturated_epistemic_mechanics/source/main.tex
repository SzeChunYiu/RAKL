\begin{abstract}
Large language models can search literature, generate hypotheses, execute analyses and draft research reports, while recent systems increasingly automate end-to-end scientific workflows. Fluency and workflow completion, however, do not determine which generated statements deserve scientific authority, whether apparently conflicting studies share a context, whether predictive agreement identifies a mechanism, or whether a self-modifying research agent improved beyond the benchmark it optimized. We introduce \RAKL{} (Recursive Atomic Knowledge Lattices), a candidate evidence-governed methodology for LLM-mediated scientific inquiry. \RAKL{} represents research as controlled updates to an external contextual Knowledge Atlas. Local scientific charts carry evidence, context and multi-axis authority certificates; typed transitions separate representation, prediction, mechanism, identification and decision claims; incompatible local views can remain a plural atlas or identified set rather than being forcibly merged. The paper makes the information lifecycle explicit: immutable evidence is contextually projected, normalized, identity-resolved, provenance-bound and inserted into the atlas; reconstructable or lossy derivative views may support retrieval and prompt compilation but never replace the canonical evidence root. A 17-stage executable lifecycle specifies exactly where an LLM may propose information and where external verification is required. In a deterministic known-answer pendulum demonstration, eight source records produced nine contextual projections and seven canonical semantic objects; context alignment prevented two false contradictions, one false mass-dependence claim remained negative history, and a missing finite-amplitude prerequisite changed the target from zero to one valid support path when new evidence arrived. The active working context used 52 of a 270-token archive estimate (19.3\%) while retaining rehydration pointers to canonical sources. The same exact software snapshot passed \SoftwareTests{} tests. These numbers demonstrate reference-engineering behavior, not scientific superiority. Strong self-evolution and real scientific-discovery claims remain preregistered for fresh assurance and a spot-first cryptocurrency study.
\end{abstract}

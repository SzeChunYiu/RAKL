\title{A Shared Structural Substrate for Data-Efficient Learning and Reasoning}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\date{10 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Large language models can encounter surface-distinct examples that instantiate the same reasoning structure and can repeatedly rediscover similar procedures at inference time. Prior work already shows benefits from skill graphs, reusable reasoning primitives and structural workflow priors, so we ask a stricter question: can one context- and quantity-of-interest-scoped structural object license cross-domain transfer, reject semantically tempting structural decoys, and serve both learning and reasoning? We introduce a directional structural witness that records role mappings, preserved invariants, non-preserved properties, target boundary conditions, evidence and uncertainty. A four-quadrant benchmark independently varies semantic and structural similarity. In the current deterministic conformance suite, the structural gate makes the intended decision on all six load-bearing Q2/Q3 cases across queue stability, positive feedback and threshold cascades, whereas a deliberately semantic-only rule makes the wrong decision on all six; all three low-semantic/high-structural transfers are licensed and all three high-semantic/low-structural decoys are rejected. This constructed result tests implementation logic, not cross-domain generalization, and the paper is not yet an efficiency result. We therefore preregister held-out transfer, training-selection and frozen-model inference experiments, with total cost including structure induction, retrieval, adaptation and verification. The shared-substrate claim fails if witnessed structure adds no value beyond strong semantic/skill controls, if Q3 false transfer remains high, if training and inference require unrelated representations, or if total cost never reaches a realistic break-even point.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Large-language-model research systems can generate hypotheses, write code, search literature and coordinate experiments, but these capabilities do not by themselves determine what a generated claim is allowed to change in a persistent scientific record. We develop an ``epistemic mechanics'' view in which scientific reasoning is represented as controlled transition over an external typed state. Claims are indexed by context, evidence, uncertainty and provenance; compatibility is mediated by explicit alignment maps; scientific authority is multi-coordinate rather than scalar; and proposal-generating models possess no direct canonical write authority. This state discipline is related to, but not reducible to, belief revision, truth-maintenance systems, abstract argumentation, database provenance or sheaf-theoretic local-to-global reasoning.

Two extensions address failures that arise even when canonical state is protected. First, a transient capacity-bounded workspace selects and broadcasts computationally useful material while remaining formally distinct from cross-context coherence and epistemic authority. We formulate the reference gate as a constrained top-$k$ problem, prove an optimality result for its reservation-first rule under the implemented assumptions, and separate computational influence from evidential provenance. Second, Open-World Mechanism Discovery (OWMD) begins from unowned functions rather than framework terminology. It constructs function signatures, executes heterogeneous retrieval routes, requires at least one lexically independent route, records citation/freshness obligations and permits only bounded discovery closure. We prove that unrestricted open-world completeness cannot be certified from any finite transcript without a complete membership oracle, and we characterize when a bounded closure certificate expires as its freshness horizon moves.

The paper also resolves an overloaded mathematical term inherited from the implementation. The current atom--witness--path object is a typed compatibility complex, not an order-theoretic lattice. We give a three-context parity counterexample showing why pairwise compatibility need not imply higher-order gluing, then state sufficient conditions under which a genuine lattice does arise: fixed points of a closure operator on a powerset form a complete lattice with intersection as meet and closure-of-union as join. Finally, a worked pendulum trace connects the formalism to an ordinary mechanics problem, including the exact elliptic-integral period and finite-amplitude expansion. The result is a long-form formal account of evidence-governed scientific state. It is not a claim of phenomenal consciousness, universal discovery, completed independent peer review or empirical scientific superiority.
\end{abstract}

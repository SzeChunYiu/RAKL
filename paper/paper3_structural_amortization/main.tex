\title{Directional Structural Witnesses for Fail-Closed Cross-Domain Transfer}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\date{11 August 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Large language models can encounter surface-distinct examples that instantiate the same reasoning structure, yet semantic analogy can also invite unsafe transfer across load-bearing boundary changes. We ask whether a context- and quantity-of-interest-scoped structural object can license cross-domain transfer while rejecting semantic decoys. We introduce a directional structural witness that records role mappings, preserved invariants, non-preserved properties, target boundaries, evidence and uncertainty. In a deterministic conformance suite, its gate makes the intended decision on all six constructed Q2/Q3 cases across queue stability, positive feedback and threshold cascades; a deliberately semantic-only rule fails all six. We then froze and ran a leave-one-family-out diagnostic on 44 internally proposed Q1--Q4 pairs across 11 families. Witness features improved ROC-AUC by 0.086 and average precision by 0.097 over the strongest of the frozen lexical/tag controls, with Q2 true accept 1.00 and Q3 false accept 0.00. These are constructed, same-session proposal labels and do not establish an advantage over a modern content encoder. A fresh 16-item natural-domain source set and an opaque, exact-subject-bound external-annotation packet are now frozen, but zero external judgements or adjudications have been received. A content-bound BGE reranker control and parent-applicability contract have also been frozen before labels, but the exact model asset is not staged and no semantic descriptor has been generated. The overall gate therefore fails closed, no expensive training or inference was authorized, and the paper is not yet an efficiency result. The result supports a reproducible diagnostic formalism, not natural cross-domain generalization or a shared learning--reasoning substrate; that stronger claim remains unlicensed until the strong-control, independent-annotation, training, inference and total cost-to-capability gates pass.
\end{abstract}

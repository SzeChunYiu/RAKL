\title{Directional Structural Witnesses for Fail-Closed Cross-Domain Transfer}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\date{10 August 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Large language models can encounter surface-distinct examples that instantiate the same reasoning structure, yet semantic analogy can also invite unsafe transfer across load-bearing boundary changes. We ask whether a context- and quantity-of-interest-scoped structural object can license cross-domain transfer while rejecting semantic decoys. We introduce a directional structural witness that records role mappings, preserved invariants, non-preserved properties, target boundaries, evidence and uncertainty. In a deterministic conformance suite, its gate makes the intended decision on all six constructed Q2/Q3 cases across queue stability, positive feedback and threshold cascades; a deliberately semantic-only rule fails all six. We then froze and ran a leave-one-family-out diagnostic on 44 internally proposed Q1--Q4 pairs across 11 families. Witness features improved ROC-AUC by 0.086 and average precision by 0.097 over the strongest dependency-aware control, with Q2 true accept 1.00 and Q3 false accept 0.00. These are constructed, same-session proposal labels: zero items have the required independent human/expert annotations or adjudication. The overall gate therefore fails closed, no expensive training or inference was authorized, and the paper is not yet an efficiency result. The result supports a reproducible diagnostic formalism, not natural cross-domain generalization or a shared learning--reasoning substrate; that stronger claim remains unlicensed until the independent-annotation, training, inference and total cost-to-capability gates pass.
\end{abstract}
